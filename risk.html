Two kinds of risk

Risk in the stock is the same for every owner: will the edge last, can the business get through a bad year, how far away is the value, how wide is the model's range, and is the price above the value. It decides whether to buy, and the fair value and margin of safety already allow for it (growth fades over the advantage period; distant cash gets a finite edge and the duration checks).

Risk to you depends on you, so it changes the position size and never the fair value. It decides how much to buy. Each size is the smallest of three answers:

  • What the return justifies: max position × edge × confidence, from the Monte Carlo range at today's price.
  • What you can afford to lose (Whitworth: reduce the bet to an affordable risk). Your loss budget ÷ the bad-case drop, so one stock's bad case costs you at most the budget. The budget is where your own situation comes in (Bernoulli: the same loss matters more when you have less).
  • Caps (Cramer: each extra dollar is worth less to you than the last, so a bigger upside should not buy a bigger bet): max position, no theme above 25% of the portfolio, and all sizes together within your stock share.

Model estimates, not advice. The range does not include the risk that the model itself is wrong, which is why the bad-case drop is never taken as less than 35%.
