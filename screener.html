How the Growth Quality Score works

A shortlist tool, not a prediction. It replaces the original growth score, which counted growth twice and let energy stocks and one-off spikes dominate.

What it measures (0 to 100), score v4

  • Business quality 30%: ROIC 35, net margin 20, gross margin 20, FCF ÷ net income 15 (statements, 3 years combined from November 2026; latest year before), ROE 10 (only when equity is meaningful).
  • Growth & consistency 30%: latest-quarter sales growth (year on year) 25, 3-year revenue CAGR 25, EPS CAGR 20, revenue-growth volatility 15 and earnings variability 15 (steadier is better).
  • Valuation 20%: forward P/E 30 and EV/EBITDA 25 (lower is better), FCF yield 30, earnings yield 15. From November 2026 the FCF yield uses statement free cash flow (last 4 quarters) instead of Yahoo's estimate, and the earnings yield uses trailing earnings, so it no longer repeats the forward P/E.
  • Financial resilience 20%: net debt/EBITDA 35, interest cover 25, FCF variability 15, negative-FCF years 15, share-count change 10.

Each metric is a percentile inside the stock's sector; each part re-weights over the metrics that exist. Cyclical sectors (Energy, Basic Materials): growth 15%, quality 35%, valuation 25%, resilience 25%. A part with no data scores at the 30th percentile. No blanket penalties: the score is capped at 50 only when FCF was negative in 2+ years and interest cover is weak.

Quick answers (Checks column)

  • Revenue growing? Yes if the latest quarter and the 3-year CAGR are both above 5%; No if both are flat or falling; Partly otherwise, or when the latest jump is far above the 3-year trend (likely an acquisition).
  • Profits healthy? Yes if net margin is at least 10%, ROIC is at or above the cost of capital and EPS isn't shrinking; No if loss-making, or thin margin and ROIC well below the cost of capital. Banks: return on equity ≥ 12%.
  • Cash flow strong? Yes if FCF was positive every year, is at least 0.8× net income and steady; No if negative in 2+ years or negative now. N/A for banks; utilities' negative FCF counts only as Partly.
  • Debt manageable? Uses the same warning and serious limits as the Risk level (higher limits for utilities and REITs). N/A for banks.
  • Financial position improving? Compares the oldest and latest annual statements: leverage, net margin, FCF growth, share count and whether growth is accelerating. Yes if improvements outnumber deteriorations by 2+, No if the reverse.

Resilient compounders (5 added after the top 50)

  • Stocks outside the top 50, worth at least $10B, that pass every absolute test: net debt ≤ 1× EBITDA (or net cash), interest cover ≥ 10× (or no interest), no negative free-cash-flow year, FCF variability ≤ 0.5, share count not growing (≤ +0.5% a year), risk level Normal, 3-year revenue growth ≥ 3% a year and business quality ≥ 60.
  • The 5 with the highest business quality are added regardless of their overall rank (one per company, so GOOG/GOOGL count once). Banks and insurers can't qualify; fee-based financials can.

Passes all 5 checks (added after the resilient picks)

  • Every stock that answers yes to all five quick questions (revenue growing, profits healthy, cash flow strong, debt manageable, financial position improving) gets a 5/5 ✓ badge.
  • Those not already in the top 50 or the resilient picks are added to the list each month, whatever their rank. Their lower score usually comes from valuation or a strong peer group, not a weak business. Use the Passes all 5 checks chip to see all of them.

Peer groups

  • Some companies are ranked against a custom peer group instead of their yfinance sector (data/peer_groups.json, editable). Fee-based financials (payments, exchanges, ratings and data firms such as V, MA, SPGI, ICE) earn fees rather than lend, so they're compared with each other and get the normal ROIC and balance-sheet tests instead of the bank treatment.

Financial Safety and Risk level

  • Financial Safety (0–100) is the resilience part shown on its own.
  • Risk level: High if any serious threshold is hit, Elevated if any warning, otherwise Normal. Serious / warning: net debt over 4× / 3× EBITDA, interest cover under 3× / 5×, negative FCF in 2+ / 1 years, shares up over 5% / 3% a year.
  • Utilities and REITs are financed with more debt by design: net debt limits 6×/7× (utilities) and 6.5×/8× (REITs), interest cover 2.5×/1.8×, and utilities' negative FCF is not counted. Banks and insurers are judged on dilution only.
  • Data confidence: High, Medium or Low from missing key metrics, borrowed multi-year data or a ROIC fallback. Industry rank and 1-year volatility, worst fall and beta are shown for context, not scored.

Multi-year figures come from ~4 years of annual statements (yfinance), so "CAGR" covers up to 3 yearly steps. Months whose CSV predates these metrics borrow them from the newest full screener run. ROIC uses operating invested capital (total assets − cash − non-debt current liabilities), so buybacks and negative book equity don't distort it. Variability = population standard deviation ÷ average, shown only when the average is positive and there are at least 3 years. WACC uses market-value equity; the cost of debt is interest ÷ average debt, limited to 2–10% with the raw figure shown. Weights are a reasoned starting point, not yet back-tested.

What is different from before

  • Every metric is ranked inside the stock's own sector, so energy is compared with energy and tech with tech.
  • Energy and Basic Materials are cyclical, so growth counts 12% and cash generation 33%.
  • Loss-making companies lose 10 points; missing P/E data loses 5.
  • Flags mark one-off growth (over 100%), loss-making and cyclical companies.
  • Consistent means the stock was in the top 50 in at least 3 of the last 4 months, including the latest. August and September 2026 are estimated (straight line between the real July and October files) and are shown with dashed borders; rank change is measured against the last real month.

"Growth priced in" labels

  • Demanding: the price assumes over 25% more growth than the company has recently delivered.
  • In line: within about 25% of recent growth.
  • Undemanding: under 75% of recent growth.
  • FCF distorted: free cash flow is under 25% of earnings (or it is a financial company), so earnings are used and there is no verdict.
  • Sales shrinking: no fair comparison, no verdict.

Market P/E

  • Total market value / total earnings of the screened stocks with positive earnings (how index P/Es are quoted), trailing and forward, recalculated each month. Each stock's drawer shows how far its P/E is above or below it.

Required returns come from one file shared with the Fair Value tab (data/required_returns.json): a stock's own rate if it has a full model (e.g. NVDA 10%), otherwise the theme rate (chips and AI hardware 11.5%), otherwise the sector rate (Damodaran Jan 2026 cost of equity + about 1 point, floor 7%: Utilities 7%, Financials and Real Estate 7.5%, Energy 8.5%, Healthcare 9%, Basic Materials 9.5%, Technology 10.5%, others 9%).
